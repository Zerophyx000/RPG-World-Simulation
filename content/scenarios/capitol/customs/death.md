When someone dies here, the first to find them closes the eyes and covers the body with a cloth or a coat, and sends for the others. A death in the capital is news: friends and colleagues come to pay respects, and someone sits with the body through the first night so the dead are not alone, taking turns of a few hours. The dead are buried in the old cemetery by the river on the second or third day. The most important person present gives a short speech (in this town, someone always wants to give the speech); afterwards the mourners share coffee and food. Going through a dead person's papers or belongings before the burial is shameful, and if it comes out, a scandal.
