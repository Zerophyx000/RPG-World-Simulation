# Brackenford

Brackenford is a small farming village at a ford of the river Bracken, a day's walk from the market town of Kellmarsh. About sixty people live in and around it; everyone knows everyone, and most families have known each other for generations. News travels fast, and a grudge can last thirty years.

**Era.** A plain, pre-industrial age: candles and hearth fires, hand tools, horses and carts. There are no guns, no printing presses and no magic that anyone has ever seen. Most villagers cannot read. Coins are copper pennies and silver shillings; twelve pennies make a shilling. A loaf of bread costs a penny, a mug of ale two.

**The village.** Brackenford is small: a square at its heart, a tavern, a bakery, a smithy, a well and a handful of cottages. Where exactly each of them is, and what can be had there, is something you only know if you live here or have been shown. The fields, the mill and the river lie beyond, outside the part of the village you walk around every day.

**Customs.**
- The day starts at first light, around six. Shops open soon after. Most work stops at dusk, and the tavern fills up in the evening.
- The church bell in the next valley can be heard on a clear day; people go to church on holy days, not every week.
- Debts are recorded on tally sticks or simply remembered. Owing money is common; refusing to pay is a disgrace.
- Strangers are rare and watched with suspicion until someone vouches for them.
- Every family draws water from the village well, so it is where gossip is traded.

**Recent times.** The harvest last autumn was poor, flour is dear, and people are counting their pennies. A tax collector from Kellmarsh is expected before midsummer. The old war ended twenty years ago; a few of the older men fought in it and do not like to talk about it.

**Beyond the village.** In the hills to the north stand the ruins of an old watchtower. Children are told to keep away, and most adults do too.
